# NON-BINDING REFERENCE MATERIAL — NOT A TEMPLATE

These examples come from the approved Klyron SA outreach specification (section 4). They teach
**commercial reasoning only**. The generator must not copy their openings, subjects or CTAs,
must not map their angles to fixed steps, must not use bracketed placeholders, and must not
assume their hypothetical events happened. Every email is written from the lead's own verified
research.

## Subject lines, CTAs and example library

Subjects should feel like legitimate operational correspondence rather than an advertising headline. They should be short, specific, grounded in the lead's real remit and never deceive about urgency, prior contact, project involvement or inside knowledge.

**Illustrative subject transformations (not prescribed):**
- Avoid: “When rework eats into project profit” → Consider: “Installation interfaces — [verified project]”
- Avoid: “Protect your projects from delays” → Consider: “Coordination before installation”
- Avoid: “The cost beyond one project” → Consider: “[Company] — project delivery”
- Avoid: “Don't lose your next client” → Consider: “Open design issues — [verified project]”
- Avoid: “Stop wasting money on rework” → Consider: “MEP / structural interfaces”

**Illustrative CTA transformations:**
- Weak: “Could we discuss our BIM services?” → Better: “Would 15 minutes this week be worthwhile to examine where late coordination could turn into paid rework?”
- Weak: “Are you interested in a call?” → Better: “Could we use 15 minutes to discuss how critical interfaces can be closed before they affect a work package?”
- Weak: “Can I show you what we do?” → Better: “Is there 15 minutes this week to explore how to prevent unresolved technical decisions from turning into contractor claims?”

### Example A — SA-CON / Project Director / financial loss
**Subject:** Installation interfaces — [verified project, if applicable]

Hi [First Name],

If a structural/MEP conflict is discovered after installation, [Company] could face more than rework: wasted materials, repeated labour and subcontractor claims against the project margin.

The expensive part is discovering it after the money has already been spent.

Would 15 minutes this week be worthwhile to examine how those interfaces could be resolved before they reach site?

### Example B — SA-PM / Managing Director / accountability and client loss
**Subject:** [Company] — unresolved design issues

The client now questions the additional costs, the missed milestone and your team's oversight. That exposure can extend beyond one project into client confidence and future appointments.

Could we spend 15 minutes this week examining how to prevent unresolved issues from becoming commercial disputes?
